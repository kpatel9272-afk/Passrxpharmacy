GPhC Mock Exam: How to Get the Most From Practice Papers

A mock exam shows you what you can do under time pressure. Here is how to sit one and, more importantly, how to learn from it.

Last updated: 6 November 2026 · Written by a practising pharmacist

Official information first. Dates, format, permitted items and eligibility are set by the GPhC and can change. Use the GPhC registration assessment pages as your source of truth; see also our 2027 exam hub.

Why mock exams are useful

  • They test recall under time pressure, which is different from reading notes.
  • They expose weak topics you did not know you had.
  • They build pacing and stamina for a long paper.
  • They make the real exam format familiar, so nerves are lower.

How to sit a mock

  1. Choose a quiet place and switch off your phone.
  2. Set a timer and keep to it. Check the GPhC site for current paper lengths and timings so your mock matches them.
  3. Do not look anything up. Guess and flag uncertain questions.
  4. Practise the calculation paper separately, with only the items you will be allowed to use. Check permitted items with the GPhC.

How to review a mock

The review is where the learning happens. Allow at least as long for review as for the paper.

  1. Sort every miss: knowledge gap, misread question, calculation slip or guess that was wrong.
  2. Look up the source (BNF, NICE guideline, legislation) for each knowledge gap.
  3. Write one line per error in your mistakes log.
  4. Re-test those topics within a week.
  5. Also check questions you got right by luck.

What makes a useful mock

  • Realistic scenarios across all areas, not just recall of facts.
  • Answers with reasoning, not only a letter.
  • Current guidance, with dated sources.
  • A mix of question types matching the exam structure.
