GPhC Registration Assessment Revision: A Practical Guide

How to organise your revision, what to prioritise, and how to turn notes into marks on exam day.

Last updated: 4 November 2026 · Written by a practising pharmacist

Official information first. Dates, format, permitted items and eligibility are set by the GPhC and can change. Use the GPhC registration assessment pages as your source of truth; see also our 2027 exam hub.

What the registration assessment is

The registration assessment is the exam trainee pharmacists in Great Britain sit as part of qualifying to register with the GPhC. It tests whether you can apply knowledge to realistic pharmacy situations, including clinical pharmacy, law and ethics, and pharmaceutical calculations. For the current structure of the papers, number of questions, timings, permitted items and attempt limits, use the GPhC's own pages. We keep a summary on our 2027 hub.

A revision strategy that works

  1. Start from the specification. Read the GPhC assessment framework and mark each area red, amber or green. Revise red first.
  2. Do questions early, not last. Active recall beats re-reading. Attempt questions on a topic the day after you revise it, then again a week later.
  3. Treat calculations as a separate skill. Speed and accuracy come from daily short sets, not one long weekend. See our worked calculation examples.
  4. Keep a mistakes log. Write each wrong answer as one line: topic, why you got it wrong, the rule you will use next time.
  5. Rehearse the exam. Sit timed papers under exam conditions. Our guide to using mock exams well covers how to review them.
  6. Plan backwards from your sitting. Work back from your exam date, and give each topic at least two passes plus a timed mock in the final weeks.

What to cover

Clinical topics

Work through the major body systems and therapeutic areas: cardiovascular, respiratory, endocrine, gastrointestinal, infections, CNS, pain, renal, musculoskeletal, women's health, haematology, dermatology and clinical pharmacy. Use current NICE guidelines, the BNF and MHRA safety updates, because guidance changes. Each of our revision note topics states what it covers.

Calculations

Doses by weight and body surface area, concentrations and dilutions, infusion rates, unit conversions and renal function estimates. Practise until the method is automatic.

Law, ethics and professional practice

Controlled drugs, legal classification of medicines, emergency supply, confidentiality, consent, safeguarding, raising concerns and the GPhC standards. Start with our law and ethics overview.

Communication and consultation skills

Even where the exam is written, safe practice depends on clear consultation and counselling. Our OSCE preparation page covers structured approaches you can practise with a colleague.
