GPhC OSCE Preparation: Building Consultation Skills

How to practise communication and clinical reasoning in a structured way, whichever form your assessment takes.

Last updated: 4 November 2026 · Written by a practising pharmacist

Check the current format. We do not make claims about the exact current GPhC assessment format. OSCE-style stations may be used in training, employer or university assessments. Check the GPhC website and your training provider for what applies to you.

What stations usually test

  • Clear, respectful communication.
  • Gathering the right information safely.
  • Clinical reasoning and safe decisions.
  • Counselling and checking understanding.
  • Professionalism and knowing your limits.

A simple consultation structure

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm the patient's identity and explain the purpose.
  2. Ask open questions first, then specific ones. Explore ideas, concerns and expectations.
  3. Check safety: red flags, allergies, other medicines, pregnancy, age.
  4. Decide and explain in plain English, in small chunks.
  5. Check understanding and invite questions.
  6. Safety-net and close: what to watch for and when to seek help.

Frameworks such as Calgary-Cambridge, SBAR and ICE (ideas, concerns, expectations) can help you remember the flow.

Example station structure

Scenario: A patient collects a first prescription for a medicine they have not taken before and asks how to take it.

StageWhat a marker looks for
OpeningIntroduces self, confirms identity, explains purpose
AssessmentAsks what they already know, checks allergies and other medicines
CounsellingDose, timing, key adverse effects, what to avoid, in plain language
CheckAsks patient to repeat back, invites questions
CloseSafety-nets, offers follow-up, documents

Common mistakes

  • Using jargon.
  • Starting with closed questions only.
  • Giving too much information at once.
  • Forgetting to check understanding.
  • Missing a red flag or an interaction.
  • Not acknowledging the patient's concern.

Preparation checklist

  • ☐ Practise out loud with a colleague, not just in your head.
  • ☐ Time yourself.
  • ☐ Learn the key counselling points for high-risk medicines.
  • ☐ Learn emergency management for common emergencies.
  • ☐ Ask for feedback on your tone and clarity.
  • ☐ Know when to refer or escalate.
