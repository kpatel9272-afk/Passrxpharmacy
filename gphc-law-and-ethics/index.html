GPhC Law and Ethics: What to Revise

A map of the law, ethics and professional practice areas candidates should be comfortable with, and how to revise them.

Last updated: 4 November 2026 · Written by a practising pharmacist

Educational overview, not official guidance. This page is a revision aid written by PassRx. It is not GPhC guidance and not legal advice. Law and standards change, so always check the legislation and the GPhC's own documents.

Legal classification of medicines

Know the difference between prescription-only medicines (POM), pharmacy (P) medicines and general sale list (GSL) medicines, who can sell or supply them, and the main exemptions. Revise from the Human Medicines Regulations 2012.

Controlled drugs

  • The Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 and the Misuse of Drugs Regulations 2001: the five schedules and what each allows.
  • Prescription requirements, validity (28 days for Schedule 2, 3 and 4) and what to do about errors.
  • Storage, record-keeping, destruction and the role of the accountable officer.
  • Supply on request or in an emergency, and which controlled drugs are excluded.

Supply of medicines

  • Emergency supply at the request of a prescriber or a patient: conditions, quantity limits and records.
  • Patient group directions and what they must contain.
  • Prescription checks: validity, legality and clinical appropriateness.
  • Responsible pharmacist regulations and their duties.

Professional standards

Learn the GPhC standards for pharmacy professionals and the standards for registered pharmacies, and be able to apply them to scenarios. The standards for pharmacy professionals are organised around person-centred care, working in partnership, communication, knowledge and skills, professional judgement, professional behaviour, confidentiality, speaking up, and leadership.

Ethics and consent

  • Confidentiality and when it can be broken.
  • Consent and the Mental Capacity Act 2005.
  • Children and young people: competence and consent.
  • Safeguarding adults and children.
  • Duty of candour and raising concerns.
  • Equality and treating people fairly.

Medicines safety and reporting

Yellow Card reporting, handling errors and near misses, and learning from incidents. Be able to say what you would do, in what order, in a short scenario.

How to revise law and ethics

  1. Make one-page summaries for each area with the key numbers and rules.
  2. Practise scenario questions: identify the principle, then the action.
  3. When unsure, choose the answer that is safest for the patient, lawful and honest.
  4. Review any law that has changed recently.
