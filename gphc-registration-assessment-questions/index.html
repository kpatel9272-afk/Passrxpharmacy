GPhC Registration Assessment Questions: Examples and Advice

Try sample single best answer and extended matching questions, read the reasoning, and learn how to approach them under time pressure.

Last updated: 4 November 2026 · Written by a practising pharmacist

Official information first. Dates, format, permitted items and eligibility are set by the GPhC and can change. Use the GPhC registration assessment pages as your source of truth; see also our 2027 exam hub.

These are original PassRx practice questions written for revision. They are not past GPhC papers.

Question styles

Single best answer (SBA): a scenario and several options where one is best. More than one option may be partly right, so choose the safest and most appropriate. Extended matching questions (EMQ): a list of options followed by several scenarios; you match each scenario to the best option. Options can be used once, more than once or not at all. Check the GPhC specification for the types used in the current paper.

Sample SBA questions

1. An adult has anaphylaxis after a wasp sting. Which is the most appropriate immediate treatment?

  1. Oral chlorphenamine 4 mg
  2. Adrenaline 500 micrograms intramuscularly
  3. Hydrocortisone 100 mg intravenously
  4. Salbutamol 100 micrograms inhaled
  5. Adrenaline 500 micrograms intravenously
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Intramuscular adrenaline (500 micrograms, 0.5 mL of 1 in 1000) into the anterolateral thigh is first-line for adults. Antihistamines and steroids are adjuncts and must not delay adrenaline. IV adrenaline is for specialist settings.

2. A patient is prescribed methotrexate for rheumatoid arthritis. Which counselling point is the most important?

  1. Take it once a day with food
  2. Take it once a week on the same day
  3. Take it only when the joints are painful
  4. Stop it if a sore throat develops, without telling anyone
  5. Avoid all fluids on dosing day
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Methotrexate for inflammatory disease is a once-weekly medicine; accidental daily dosing has caused fatalities. Sore throat, fever, mouth ulcers or unexplained bruising need urgent medical advice (not self-stopping without advice).

3. For how long is a prescription for a Schedule 2 controlled drug valid?

  1. 7 days
  2. 14 days
  3. 28 days
  4. 56 days
  5. 6 months
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Prescriptions for Schedule 2, 3 and 4 controlled drugs are valid for 28 days from the date on the prescription (or the start date specified, where later). Always check current legislation for the full prescription requirements.

4. A child weighing 20 kg needs 15 mg/kg of a drug. The suspension contains 125 mg in 5 mL. What volume gives one dose?

  1. 6 mL
  2. 9 mL
  3. 12 mL
  4. 15 mL
  5. 18 mL
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Dose = 15 × 20 = 300 mg. 300 ÷ 125 × 5 mL = 12 mL.

Sample EMQ

Options: A Amlodipine · B Ramipril · C Bisoprolol · D Furosemide · E Spironolactone

Choose the best drug for each statement.

  1. Persistent dry cough is a recognised adverse effect.
  2. Ankle swelling is a common dose-related adverse effect.
  3. Raises the risk of hyperkalaemia when added to an ACE inhibitor.
Show answers and explanation

1 = B, 2 = A, 3 = E. ACE inhibitors such as ramipril cause a dry cough through bradykinin accumulation. Dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers such as amlodipine cause peripheral oedema. Spironolactone is potassium-sparing, so combining it with an ACE inhibitor needs potassium and renal monitoring.

How to approach exam questions

  • Read the last line first so you know what is being asked, then read the scenario.
  • Look for the safety cue: renal function, pregnancy, allergy, interacting drugs, age and weight.
  • Cover the options and form your own answer before reading them.
  • Eliminate options that are unsafe, outdated or do not answer the question.
  • Flag and move on if stuck. Keep to time per question.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing an option that is true but not the best answer for this patient.
  • Missing units, such as micrograms and milligrams, or mL per hour and mL per minute.
  • Relying on old guidance. Check the current NICE guideline or BNF entry.
  • Reading the scenario too fast and missing a renal, hepatic or pregnancy detail.

Using practice questions effectively

Do small sets by topic, then mixed timed papers. After each set, read every explanation (including questions you got right), and add each error to a mistakes log. Repeat the same topics after a gap. Quantity matters less than reviewing properly.
